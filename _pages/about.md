**Trustworthy AI | Computer Vision | AI for Science**

I am a **Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellow at Cornell University**, where I develop trustworthy and interpretable AI for scientific and engineering applications. My research spans explainable machine learning, computer vision, scientific modeling, and the reliability of AI systems in real-world environments.

My doctoral research at **Zhejiang University** focused on explainable AI and reliable machine learning. I received my Ph.D. in 2023. Before joining Cornell, I was a Research Fellow at **Nanyang Technological University**, working with Continental on multi-robot planning and the robustness of multi-agent learning systems.

At Cornell, my current projects include rooftop photovoltaic (PV) mapping and deployment analysis, single-image 3D plant reconstruction, and agentic workflows for scientific image analysis. Across these projects, I am interested in how AI can turn image and sensor data into evidence that researchers can interpret and validate. This involves adapting models across domains, preserving uncertainty in incomplete observations, and connecting model outputs with the scientific questions they are intended to answer.

## Research Interests

- **AI for energy and PV analysis:** Rooftop PV segmentation, spatial matching, and longitudinal analysis of solar deployment. My recent studies examine household and large-roof adoption pathways, deployment across border cities, and the stock and flow of rooftop solar across U.S. metropolitan areas.
- **Computer vision and 3D reconstruction:** Vision Transformers, convolutional neural networks, semantic segmentation, domain adaptation, monocular depth estimation, and single-view 3D reconstruction for scientific and engineering applications.
- **Scientific AI:** Vision-language models and agentic workflows that coordinate foundation models with specialized analytical tools for large-scale image analysis.
- **Explainable and reliable machine learning:** General feature-attribution methods, model interpretation, adversarial robustness, model verification, and fault diagnosis under data scarcity and distribution shifts.
